# Course 3 · Chapter 3.3: Cost allocation: fixed subscriptions to token share, distributions, the unattributed share

_Andrey Popov's project notes: how it works, why it was built this way, and the theory behind it._

**In short:** How a fixed monthly AI tool subscription is allocated to tickets by cache-read token share, reported as median, quartiles and p90 with an explicit unattributed share. Allocated plus unattributed equals the bill; delivered tickets spanned about 170x.

## 2. The problem (business view, plain English)

The team pays a fixed monthly fee for each AI tool seat. The invoice says nothing about which ticket used how much. A manager asking "what does a delivered change cost?" gets either "the fee" (useless) or a guess. I wanted a defensible way to spread the fixed fee over the work that consumed it, show the spread between tickets, and say plainly how much could not be assigned.

Finance analogy: shared-service overhead allocation. A head-office cost is charged to business units by an allocation key (headcount, floor area, revenue). The key is a convention. It makes unit P&Ls add up to the group total, but it does not say what each unit "caused". Mine is the same: the key is each ticket's share of cache-read tokens, and the sum of allocated plus unattributed equals the bill exactly.

## 4. How it works (technical walkthrough)

```
 Claude logs ─► token-spend.py ─► rows (licence, scope, ticket, model) × 4 token buckets ┐
 Codex logs  ─► codex-spend.py ─► rows (…) × cached/fresh/output                          │ tokens only,
                                                                                          │ "not currency"
 fixed charges (user-provided) ───────────────────────┐                                   ▼
 ticket attribution: direct (dir) / inferred (text) / unattributed          ┌─ manual allocation ─┐
 delivery context (merged PRs, ticket state) ───────────────────────────────►  share of cache-read │
                                                                            │  per tool, per pool  │
                                                                            └──────────┬──────────┘
                                                      allocated per ticket + unattributed = bill
                                                      report: n, median, Q1–Q3, p90, range, by delivery state
```

**Step 1: usage rows.** For each ticket, window and model, sum the four Claude buckets (fresh input, output, cache write, cache read) or the Codex buckets. Buckets are never summed together: rates differ by more than an order of magnitude, and a single "total tokens" figure is about 97% cache reads and carries no cost signal.

**Step 2: attribution classes.** *Direct*: the session's working-directory name contains a ticket key (`classify`, *TS.py:93–102*). *Inferred*: a text search of the transcript for ticket keys; the dominant ticket wins only with at least 5 hits and at least a 50% share (`infer_ticket_from_bytes`, *TS.py:133–147*). *Unattributed*: everything else. For Claude in the 19 August validation snapshot, about two-thirds of cache-read was direct and about one-ninth inferred. Codex has no direct path in the August result: its rows are content-inferred.

**Step 3: pools and driver.** Combine the two Claude charges, split by each pool's share of Claude cache-read tokens. Split the Codex charge by each pool's share of Codex cached-input tokens. A pool is a ticket, or a bucket such as "dimensioned but unattributed" or "known subagent remainder".

**Step 4: identity check.** Allocated to ticket rows plus unattributed equals the billed total exactly (difference 0 in the snapshot). I verified the arithmetic myself: the per-state sums (delivered, open, closed without delivery, measurement work) equal the allocated total, and the Claude and Codex bucket weights sum to the measured cache-read totals.

**Step 5: report.** Count, median, Q1–Q3, p90, min–max for delivered tickets, with PR size bands, tool mix and attribution class per row. Open and non-delivering spend is shown by state, not dropped. In the August result the 12 delivered tickets span a range of about 170× from smallest to largest, Q3/Q1 is about 7×, p90/median is about 3×, and the all-rows median is lower than the delivered median. That spread is the finding; an average would hide it.

**What the scripts add around it.** Half-open UTC windows on record timestamps (not file mtime); frozen snapshots with SHA-256; diagnostics counters; a population audit script that recomputes event and token totals independently. Chapter 3.4 covers those.

## 5. Why it was built this way (decisions and trade-offs)

| Decision | Alternatives | Why this one | Cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allocate the fixed fee by cache-read share | Public list price × tokens; output tokens; time spent; story points | The fee is fixed and no itemised rates exist; list price would be "as though it were the account's cost"; cache-read exists for the whole population ||
| One key per tool; never add tokens across tools | One combined key | Buckets and semantics differ; Codex normalisation is an assumption | Cannot say which tool is "cheaper" |
| Keep unattributed dollars visible | Spread them over tickets | Forcing them would overstate ticket cost | The headline looks less complete |
| Distribution with size context | Mean cost per PR or per point | Heterogeneous units; the lead's critique | Needs n and population every time |
| Label "allocated actual spend" | "Ticket cost" | A fixed fee has no per-ticket charge | Less quotable |
| Report cost-incurred period and delivery cut-off | One label | Spend in month P may deliver in P+1 | Cohorts move between reports |
| Manual monthly allocation | A scheduled pipeline | Billing and delivery inputs arrive by hand; scope decisions needed ||

## 6. Theory behind it (the "lecture" part)

### 6.1 Joint and fixed cost allocation
A **fixed cost** does not vary with volume; a **variable cost** does. When one fixed cost serves many cost objects, any split is a convention. Common keys: proportional to usage (drivers), to benefit, to ability to bear, or equal. In **activity-based costing**, the key should be the *driver* that best correlates with the cost: resource use, not convenience. Properties wanted: the shares add to 100% (full reconciliation), the key is measurable for every object, and it is hard to game.

Allocated cost is an **average (fully absorbed)** cost. **Marginal** cost is what changes if you do one more unit. For a flat subscription the marginal cost is about zero until the seat limit is hit, and the average falls as volume rises.

*In my project:* cache-read share is a usage driver chosen for availability; the output is an average cost, so I call it "allocated actual spend".

### 6.2 Measurement-scale rules: never add what is not additive
Quantities in different units cannot be summed; quantities with different rates cannot be summed before pricing. Convert to a common unit (money) first, then add. Also watch non-additive counts: the number of sessions cannot be added across two time chunks because one session can appear in both.

*In my project:* "Never add these token buckets together. The only cross-surface total in this result is money after allocating each subscription independently".

### 6.3 Distributions, quantiles and their definitions
For skewed data (cost per task is usually right-skewed with a long tail), the mean is dominated by the tail and the median is robust. The **interquartile range** Q3 − Q1 measures spread around the median. A **percentile** p of n values has several definitions:
- **Nearest rank:** the k-th smallest with k = ceil(p·n). Always an observed value.
- **Linear interpolation** (the common spreadsheet and numpy default): a weighted average of two neighbours.
For n = 12, nearest-rank p90 is the 11th value (k = ceil(10.8)). For n < 10 it equals the maximum (the PR report states this too, *ADO report caveats*). Small-n percentiles have wide uncertainty; a bootstrap confidence interval would show it.

Mixed definitions (nearest rank for Q1, Q3, p90; midpoint for the median) are not wrong but should be stated, as they were.

*In my project:* n = 12 and one developer: the distribution is a description of one month, not an estimate of a population.

### 6.4 Residuals and reconciliation identities
A good allocation has an explicit **residual**: the part you could not assign. The identity allocated + unattributed = total must hold exactly, and it must hold after rounding (rows rounded to cents, reconciled "after per-row cent rounding", *the September allocation result file*). Reporting the residual stops over-claiming. If it shrinks only because the pool was redefined, it signals nothing about quality.

### 6.5 Attribution evidence: direct, inferred, unattributed
When the key is not recorded, you infer it. Inference has a precision/recall trade-off. A strict rule (at least 5 mentions and at least a 50% share) leaves more unattributed but fewer wrong assignments; a lax rule raises coverage and error. Always report the classes separately and never present inferred as measured.

*In my project:* the thresholds are constants in the collector; the sources do not report how they were chosen or tested for precision.

### 6.6 Population, scope and validity
A ratio is meaningful only if numerator and denominator cover the same population. One developer's usage over a team's bill, or a team's tickets over one developer's usage, gives a meaningless number. State the population every time.

*In my project:* usage is one developer's local logs; the ticket population is the single repository; the Copilot seat, web or desktop use and other people are outside.

## 8. Glossary

| Term | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| Allocated actual spend | A fixed charge split over tickets by a stated key; not a directly observed ticket charge |
| Allocation driver / key | The quantity that sets each object's share (here cache-read tokens) |
| Cache-read tokens | Input tokens served from the prompt cache; the dominant bucket by volume |
| Bucket | One of the separate token counters: fresh input, output, cache write, cache read |
| Direct / inferred / unattributed | How a session was tied to a ticket: by directory name, by transcript text, or not at all |
| Unattributed share | Money (or tokens) that could not be assigned to any ticket row |
| Median, Q1–Q3, p90 | Middle value, middle half's bounds, and the value below which 90% of rows fall |
| Nearest rank | Percentile rule that returns an observed value: k = ceil(p·n) |
| Marginal vs average cost | Extra cost of one more unit vs total cost divided by units |
| Modeled amount | A planning number not backed by a verified charge |
| Pool | A group of rows sharing one driver denominator (a tool, a licence, a state) |
| Cost-incurred period | The month in which the spend happened |
